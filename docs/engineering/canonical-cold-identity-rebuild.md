# Rebuild the retained hot Fact identity registry

The first cutover changes which Fact versions require a relational identity. Keep the existing registry and its indexes until the replacement is proven and the independent rollback endpoint has been restored successfully. The single cutover approval binds the original and staged registry OIDs and the exact retirement scope. PostgreSQL can return the old files only after the cutover transaction commits.

## Preparation and transaction order

Use this implementation only through the qualified cutover operator and its writer fence. [Registry rebuild primitives](../../scripts/db/canonical_cold_identity_rebuild.py)

1. Before the writer fence, invoke the suffix preparation with a caller-owned `REPEATABLE READ` transaction and the exact database system identifier and OID. The cutoff is a storage day, never event time. The private staged regular table copies only the independently witnessed hot suffix. Every row must match the existing identity and header on ID, storage day, series, exact observation key, and revision. Preparation keeps the heap and the three uniqueness indexes in their reviewed source tablespaces. It adds the bounded retirement access path `ix_cold_hot_identity_day_id` in the same tablespace as the existing ID/day index. Record the staged and old OIDs, cutoff, source owner, temporary actual blocks and WAL peak.
2. Under the final exclusive lifecycle and writer fence, convert all four active/prepared archive FK roots as planned and detach or drop only the independently audited old header and payload sources. The serving roots must have no rows before the chosen cutoff. Review all other OID dependencies. The source and destination cannot share an unreviewed publication, RLS, custom ACL, rule or structural difference.
3. Run the swap inside that same transaction and pass the audited legacy header OID. It acquires targeted `NOWAIT` locks, copies late accepted suffix identities and verifies all three sets together. Its catalog check verifies that the real current incoming roots resolve to their original validated shape and no unknown root points to the old identity. The retained operational parent must have neither partitions nor physical storage. The swap explicitly rebinds validated serving FKs and the approved retained-parent FK to the replacement OID. PostgreSQL builds inherited FKs through their roots. The swap restores the original identity trigger enables and constraint/index names, verifies guard and placement shape, and leaves the exact previous OID closed against writes in the staging schema. The caller controls rollback and commit, including the surrounding archive authority and recovery boundary.
4. Rehearse rollback. Before commit, aborting the transaction restores the original OIDs, FK bindings, naming, and surrounding authority. Once committed or new writes are accepted, use the independently restored paired point and exact replay as documented by the cutover runbook. The relocated old identity relation alone cannot roll back an activated cold reader or recover retired headers.
5. After fenced candidate parity passes, retire the previous identity relation in the same activation transaction. Use the independent recovery digest captured at the final writer boundary. The stored swap receipt must match the database, cutoff, previous OID and serving OID. The operator checks remaining dependencies and uses `DROP TABLE RESTRICT`. Check the durable retirement receipt and previous relation's absence before opening the database to writers. An interrupted transaction restores the original and staged OIDs. A repeat with an identical stored receipt is idempotent.

The final reconciliation and rebuilding of validated FKs can scan the retained suffix while the writer fence is held. The synthetic regression does not certify that the current transaction fits a production outage window. Measure retained-set reconciliation, FK work, WAL, peak allocation, and wallet/collector/reader compatibility on the representative restored candidate. If the fence time does not fit, build and validate candidate FK roots before the final fence with an independently tested retained-delta protocol.

## Capacity boundary

On the November 14 source inventory the two SSD identity indexes were about 60.253 GB in total. The HDD registry heap and natural-key index brought the total across both devices to roughly 160.404 GB. Shrinking those HDD files yields no SSD benefit. For the October 31 sizing example the retained registry was estimated at 48.845 million rows from a registry estimated at 317.566 million. A proportional estimate returns at most about 51 GB of SSD space after replacing the old two indexes, before subtracting the new age-out index and compaction-density differences. Record the actual old and new allocated files and identify the device before assigning any SSD credit.

The independent 382.211 GB legacy header, 152.917 GB dated header, and 261.677 GB dated payload figures at that cutoff are gross conditional inventory, not prior reclaimed capacity. The identity rebuild concerns only the split-device identity allocation. It does not grant retirement authority for those cohorts.
